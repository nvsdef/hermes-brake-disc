# Rotor lessons - what went wrong before, and why the rules exist

Each section is a failure the predecessor measured. The numbers illustrate the
trap. They are not acceptance values; PRD section 4 is.

## 1. The micron-sliver failure (why vane ends are cut, never fitted)

The single most expensive geometry bug in the predecessor project.

**What happened.** The vane profile's end arcs were fitted to the ring radii and
landed at r = 164.998 and r = 94.992 against rings of exactly 165.000 and 95.000.
That left 2 um and 8 um sliver faces between each vane and the cheek cylinders.

**Why no gate saw it.**

- Volume: 2 um on a 100 mm vane is 0.002 %. The 1 % gate passed.
- Zero-area faces: a 2 um x 12 mm face is 2.4e-8 m^2. Not zero. That gate passed.

**What it did downstream.** Fluent Meshing surface meshing emitted

```
CONFORMAL_TESSELLATION_FAILED
node insertion failed at (108.661 124.16 -6)
SIGSEGV at Node 0
```

(108.661, 124.16, -6) is r = 165.000, z = -6: the vane / cheek / OD junction.
The workflow then died three tasks later with a bare "An error occurred during
task update". Nothing in that chain names the geometry.

**The fix, now the rule.** Draw the flanks past the band (r = 80 and r = 172)
and trim with two cut features at exactly r = 95 and r = 165. The end faces are
then generated by the cut and coincide with the cheek cylinders by construction.
The good build measured vane-end edges at r = 165.000000 exactly and a shortest
vertex-to-vertex chord of 3.000 mm with zero degenerate faces.

**General rule.** Never let a fitted curve define a face that has to coincide
with another feature's face. Generate both from the same nominal number. And
check slivers separately from volume.

## 2. Why the rotor is four separate bodies

The solver spins only `cheek_out` and `cheek_in` with a solid-motion model. That
term conserves energy only if the rotating body is a perfect body of revolution:
on every rotating wall the velocity must be tangent to the surface (v . n = 0).
The predecessor measured the leak, as the integral of |v . n| dA over the
rotating boundary (m^3/s per rad/s):

| Rotating zone | Integral of abs(v . n) dA | Energy accounted |
|---|---|---|
| merged rotor (rejected) | 5.65e-3 | **7.95 %** |
| split cheeks | 3.79e-6 | **99.86 %** |

The merged rotor leaked because 98.3 % of its rotating wall area stood in the
plane of rotation: about 80 % the vane flanks, 18.2 % the cross-drilled hole
bores. Splitting the rotor removes both: the flanks leave because the vanes stop
rotating, and the bores cannot exist on a body of revolution.

So:

- **Split exactly at the flat planes z = +/-6.** Flat interfaces normal to the
  axis have v . n = 0. The predecessor's old build extruded the vanes 16 mm
  across the 12 mm gap; that 2 mm overlap into each cheek is gone.
- **The hat meets `cheek_in` on the r = 95 cylinder.** A cylinder about the axis
  also has v . n = 0. Measured: hat r max 95.0000, full cylinder over z = -16 to
  -6, area 5 969.03 mm^2, equal to `cheek_in`'s bore face.
- **Between separate bodies, faces are exactly coincident: no gap, no overlap.**
  Shared topology then gives coupled thermal interfaces. The old "overlap, do
  not abut" rule applies only to two features merging **inside one part**, where
  a flush union can leave zero-area faces. Confusing the two rules is what
  produced the merged rotor.
- Expected rotor-to-rotor interfaces: 40 vane/cheek faces (604.30 mm^2 each) and
  one hat/cheek_in cylinder (5 969.03 mm^2). The hat touches the vanes only along
  an edge (r = 95, z = -6), never a face.

## 3. Why the 40-hole design model is banned

The predecessor also built `rotor_design_model.SLDPRT`: one merged body with
cross-drilled holes, so GEO-05 had
"something to verify against". It was rendered in every review package. It was
**never meshed and never solved.**

What that cost:

- The review package showed geometry nobody analysed.
- One run built corrected geometry into the design model that differed from the
  analysis model, and nothing enforced agreement. The checksum gate hashed a
  fixed list of eleven analysis files; the design model was a twelfth.
- Its merged single-body construction is the same construction that destroyed
  92 % of the injected energy in the solver.
- Two models each passing their own closed form (1.80177657e-3 against
  1.77915710e-3 m^3, 1.27 % apart) with no gate able to compare them.

H1 decision (docs/DECISIONS.md item 2, PRD section 7): **one geometry.** GEO-05
is **DEFERRED** to Phase 3 with a report row. Over a single stop, convection
removed under 1 % of the injected energy in the predecessor's solves, so
cross-drilling cannot move a single-stop verdict even if modelled. The hole
pattern is Phase 3 design intent, not a deliverable. Do not build it, render it,
sketch it "for reference", or save it in the run folder.

If you are asked for holes, the answer is the GEO-05 row: deferred, rationale,
owner Chassis / Foundation Brake, target Phase 3. You return it to the lead as a
routing error.

## 4. The circular-pattern trap

`create_pattern(pattern_type='circular')` on the vane feature reported
`instances: 20, distinct_instances: 20` and produced **one** body. Only mass
properties caught it. The working build puts all 20 profiles in one sketch from
rotated coordinates and extrudes once. PRD 3.2 makes this mandatory. The same
applies to the five bolt holes: five circles in one sketch, one cut.

## 5. The feature-graph trap

`submit_feature_graph` accepted every placement key and honoured none. The first
`cheek_out` built that way had exactly 5.7177e-4 m^3 and its centroid at
y = 5 mm. **Volume is invariant under translation.** Only the centroid saw it.
This is why every body is gated on centroid and bbox as well as volume.

## 6. The twin shortcut

`cheek_in` is `cheek_out` shifted by -22 mm. The predecessor tried copying the
file and editing the plane offset. `modify_dimension` returned `COMPLETED` with
`{requested: -0.016, effective: 0.006}`: the part did not move, and the volume
gate would have passed it. PRD section 2 now requires the negative-side parts to
be built independently.

| rotor total | 1.801760e-3 m^3 | 1.801777e-3 m^3 | same reason |
| hat outer radius | 97 on the retired design model | 95 | retired geometry |
| hat web top | z = -3 on the retired design model | z = -6 | inlet fully open |

## 8. Run 1 (FR330V-20260930-1012): right geometry, slow build

All 23 rotor bodies passed PRD section 4 exactly, but the build was slow. From
the file times:

| Part | Finished | Minutes | Where the time went |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cheek_out`, `cheek_in` | 10:20, 10:21 | about 7 | session start and two simple parts |
| `vanes` | 10:35 | about 14 | the first vane sketch used coordinates rotated by hand in the loop; arcs on vanes about 17 to 20 were crossed or mis-paired. The child then wrote a rotation script, ran it with bare `python`, hit the Windows Store stub, and had to find another interpreter |
| `hat` | 10:40 | about 5 | |

What changed for H1.2:

- **Data cards.** Every vane, trim, hat and hole sketch is a paste-ready
  `segments` string in `sketch-data.md`, generated and checked once. The agent
  copies it. Nothing is rotated or rounded during a build.
- **One trim cut** (three concentric circles, cut by parity) replaces the two
  trim cuts.
- **Hole cut from `Front Plane`**, mid-plane 0.100: no offset plane is needed,
  because the only hat material at the bolt radius is the flange.
- **Measure once per part** with `analyze_model`. Every intermediate feature is
  checked against the card with the free `result_geometry.volume`.
- **Python rule.** Never type bare `python`. Use `execute_code`, or
  `C:/solidpilot/.venv/Scripts/python.exe`.

Target for the four rotor parts: about 36 tool calls and 5 to 6 minutes.
